    28 August 2026, 2:30 pm NPT updated

    Toll holds at 489, but 393 of the bodies are still unidentified

    Nepal Police put the confirmed toll at 489 dead as of a midday bulletin on Friday, up from 469 at 7am and 389 at 9pm Thursday. ABC News, citing police, reported the same 489 figure again in a Friday afternoon update, so the toll has held since midday. Of those 489 bodies, 393 have not yet been identified, Nepal Police said, Onlinekhabar reports. The flood hit Timure in Rasuwa on Wednesday morning and ran down the Bhote Koshi into the Trishuli and the Narayani.

    Rescue counts vary by agency and by day. Police said 1,545 people caught in the flood had been rescued as of the 7am bulletin, and NDRRMA told ABC News on Friday afternoon that 3,253 people have now been rescued by helicopter across the flood zone, a still broader tally that has not been named or broken down. NDRRMA's own earlier, named list stands at 113. The Nepali Army separately said it had airlifted 449 people to safety on Friday alone, as of 12:20pm, including 13 Indian and 4 German nationals, Onlinekhabar reports. Nepal Tourism Board said 117 stranded tourists have now been rescued from the Bhotekoshi area, 85 of them Indian, 16 Chinese, 9 South Korean, 2 American and 2 Italian, with 3 not yet identified by nationality, Onlinekhabar reports. Twenty eight police personnel remain out of contact.

    Friday's midday bulletin did not repeat a district breakdown. The last full count, from the 7am bulletin, was Chitwan 178, Nawalparasi East 110, Gorkha 44, Nuwakot 37, Dhading 33, Tanahun 28, Nawalparasi West 27 and Rasuwa 12, adding up to that morning's total of 469. Health facilities in Chitwan, which has the most bodies, can hold only around 50 at a time, which is slowing identification and release to families.

    NDRRMA's 6:20pm update on Thursday listed 910 people out of contact in Nepal, up from 826 that morning, 570 of them tourists. Nepal's tourism board says 517 of the missing are foreign nationals, from 33 countries. Out of contact does not mean dead. Phone lines, power and internet are down across the valley and are coming back slowly.

    The barrier lake upstream that China's Ministry of Water Resources had been watching near the Chhochen Khola and Purepu Tsangpo has now burst. See the update above on the rescue pause and the new NDRRMA warning for the Bhotekoshi. USGS first read the shaking on Wednesday as an earthquake, then found no earthquake took place: the signal came from an ice and rock avalanche that fell into the Lhende Khola on the Tibet side, dammed the river, then broke.

    Nepal's Army says more than 5,000 army and police personnel are working the flood zone, from Nuwakot to Chitwan. Prime Minister Balendra Shah travelled to Bidur in Nuwakot on Thursday to see the damage.

    In India, the NDRF and the Sashastra Seema Bal have rescue teams on standby in Bihar and Uttar Pradesh, and the NDRF says the Gandak, which the Narayani becomes downstream, could see higher water levels. Nepal has turned down offers of search and rescue teams from India, China, the US, Britain, Japan, South Korea, Sri Lanka and Bangladesh, the Kathmandu Post reports. The government says its own army and police can handle the operation and is asking instead for cash, through a single channel into the Prime Minister's Disaster Relief Fund.

    Keep off the banks of the Bhote Koshi, the Trishuli and the Narayani until officials say it is clear.

    Sources: Nepal Police, Onlinekhabar, Onlinekhabar, Onlinekhabar, Onlinekhabar, Onlinekhabar, ABC News Australia, The Kathmandu Post, ICIMOD, Business Standard, ANI, The Kathmandu Post

    28 August 2026, 2:00 pm NPT

    Cabinet orders relief package, ministers to give a month's pay

    Nepal's Cabinet made three flood relief decisions at its Thursday meeting, government spokesperson and Education Minister Sasmita Pokharel said on Friday, Onlinekhabar reports.

    The Prime Minister and every member of the Council of Ministers will give one month's salary to the Prime Minister's Disaster Relief Fund.

    The Cabinet approved Agriculture Inputs Company Limited to buy 25,000 tonnes of DAP fertiliser from India, government to government, to help farmers who lost supplies in the flood.

    The Cabinet has told the Finance Ministry to prepare a relief and rehabilitation package for people and businesses affected by the flood in Rasuwa. The details of that package have not been published yet.

    Sources: Onlinekhabar

    28 August 2026, 1:15 pm NPT updated

    Barrier lake breach confirmed, Tibet rescue work resumes

    Nepal Police issued an alert on Friday saying Chinese authorities reported that the barrier lake near the Chhochen Khola and the Purepu Tsangpo, just across the border from Rasuwa, has burst. Rasuwa's Chief District Officer said a circular has gone out telling people along the riverbanks to stay safe, Onlinekhabar reports.

    The Nepali Army suspended rescue operations along the border for one and a half hours as a precaution. Chinese authorities separately halted their own rescue work in Gyirong county and pulled teams back a kilometre from the site, citing the risk of a second wave, ABC News reports.

    The Kathmandu Post reported early Friday afternoon that the lake has now breached. NDRRMA said water levels in the Bhotekoshi surged as a result, and helicopters carried out aerial reconnaissance over Rasuwa to check the impact downstream.

    Chinese state media said Friday afternoon that rescue operations in Tibet have resumed, after water levels at the lake dropped slightly, ABC News reports.

    NDRRMA said separately on Friday that sediment laden water is rising again in the Bhotekoshi, two days after the first flood hit the same river. Spokesperson Shanti Mahat urged people living along the Bhotekoshi, and rescue and relief workers in the area, to stay on high alert and move to safer ground immediately if they see any sign of danger, Onlinekhabar reports.

    This is a new and separate risk on top of the flood that has already killed hundreds. If you live or are working along the Bhote Koshi in Rasuwa, move away from the riverbank now and wait for officials to say it is clear.

    Sources: Onlinekhabar, Onlinekhabar, The Kathmandu Post, ABC News Australia

    28 August 2026, 11:30 am NPT

    Nepal's missing count rises to 977, NDRRMA says

    NDRRMA said on Friday morning that 977 people are now listed out of contact in Nepal, up from 910 the evening before. NDRRMA put the foreign national count at 517, the same as the previous update, IANS reports.

    The new figure has not changed Nepal Police's confirmed death toll, which stood at 489 as of Friday's midday bulletin.

    Out of contact does not mean dead. Phone lines, power and internet are still down across large parts of the flood zone, and officials are still working through the lists as contact is slowly restored.

    If you are trying to find someone in the flood zone, call the nearest District Police Office or 100, and register the same details with the local ward office.

    28 August 2026, 8:00 am NPT updated

    Army pulls 350 from Upper Trishuli tunnel, more still inside

    The Nepali Army has pulled more than 350 workers and local villagers out of a flooded access tunnel at the under construction Upper Trishuli-1 hydropower project, rescuers rappelling from a hovering helicopter to reach the entrance, Bloomberg reports, citing Nepal's Prime Minister's Office. More than 100 people were still believed trapped inside as of Thursday, and the operation is continuing.

    The Nepali Army said separately that its units have rescued more than 300 people across the flood zone so far, spokesperson Brigadier General Raja Ram Basnet said, a wider tally than NDRRMA's earlier, named count of 113. It is not confirmed how these counts relate to each other. Around two dozen helicopters and nearly 1,000 personnel from the Nepal Police, Nepali Army and Armed Police Force are working the search, the Kathmandu Post reports.

    At Trishuli 3A in Mailung, where 35 workers were reported cut off on Wednesday with the tunnel gate blocked, the army airlifted three people out on Thursday, Onlinekhabar reports. It has not said how many remain inside.

    At the Langtang Hydropower project, more than 25 workers have been out of contact in a tunnel since Wednesday. Bijay Mohan Bhattarai, the company's managing director and a secretary of IPPAN, said government agencies need to step up rescue efforts there, and that all available helicopters had already been sent elsewhere, Onlinekhabar reports.

    The Independent Power Producers' Association Nepal, IPPAN, says 215 staff from five hydropower projects in total have not been reached since Wednesday's flood. Worst hit is Upper Trishuli-1 itself, with 94 people out of contact by IPPAN's count, a narrower figure than the more than 350 the army says it has now brought out of that project's tunnel. At Rasuwagadhi, 51 staff are out of contact, though 3 reached higher ground on their own.

    Sources: Bloomberg, Onlinekhabar, Onlinekhabar, The Kathmandu Post, Onlinekhabar

    Prithvi Highway still blocked, reopening targeted for Friday

    The Prithvi Highway, the main road between Kathmandu and Pokhara, has been blocked since Wednesday evening after Bhotekoshi floodwater dumped mud across it in Gajuri and Galchhi, in Dhading, Onlinekhabar reports.

    Debris still has to be cleared at three sites: Galoudi and Bahuni Bhir in Gajuri, and Mahesh Dobhan in Galchhi. Traffic police superintendent Dilli Narayan Pandey said clearing near settlements is what is causing the delay.

    Authorities are aiming to reopen the highway by the end of Friday.

    02. Timeline

    What happened

    Pieced together from police, local officials, and news reports. Times are approximate.

    08:37 NPT

    Seismic stations picked up a signal near the border. The US Geological Survey first called it a magnitude 4.4 earthquake, then revised it: the signal was a magnitude 5.2 glacial collapse, not a quake.

    ~09:00 NPT

    The Bhotekoshi River suddenly rose at the border. It wasn't raining nearby, so plain rain wasn't the cause.

    Morning

    Timure and Syabrubesi, two border towns, were hit hardest. Nine bank branches were swept away. The local helipad was destroyed, which slowed rescue by air.

    Same day

    The flood reached towns further downstream: Betrawati and Trishuli Bazar, Galchhi and Dhunge Bazaar, then past Muglin into the Narayani. 14 hydropower projects were damaged.

    Ongoing

    Nepal's Army, Police, and Armed Police are searching for survivors with helicopters. China has confirmed deaths on its side too.

    03. Cause

    What may have caused it

    No government has confirmed the cause yet. It was not raining nearby, so ordinary rain is ruled out. Best current read, from experts reviewing satellite images:

    1. Leading explanation

      Ice and rock fell into a mountain stream and dammed it

      Nepal's Department of Hydrology and Meteorology and independent glaciologists, using satellite images shared through ICIMOD, say an ice-and-rock avalanche blocked the Lhende Khola about 20 km upstream of the Miteri Bridge. That made a temporary lake behind the debris, which then burst. They call it preliminary.

    2. Also possible

      A glacial lake burst

      A lake of meltwater held back by ice can break free on its own, with no rain. Over 20 lakes in Nepal are rated dangerous, and many more sit unmonitored across the border.

    3. Ruled out

      It was not an earthquake

      The US Geological Survey first logged a magnitude 4.4 earthquake at 08:37, minutes before the water rose. It has since revised that: the signal was a magnitude 5.2 glacial collapse, not a tectonic quake. The tremor was the collapse being recorded, not a separate trigger.

    04. Questions

    Common questions about the Nepal flood

    The things people are searching for most, answered short.

    What happened in Rasuwa, Nepal?

    On the morning of 26 August 2026 a flash flood came down the Bhote Koshi river from the Tibet side of the border into Rasuwa district. Timure, Syabrubesi, the Rasuwagadhi border crossing and around a dozen riverside settlements took the heaviest impact, and the water carried on down the Trishuli into the Narayani. The Nepal Electricity Authority reports 14 hydropower projects damaged, about 748 MW.

    What caused the Nepal flood of August 2026?

    No government has confirmed a cause. The leading explanation, from Nepal's Department of Hydrology and Meteorology and independent glaciologists working with satellite imagery shared through ICIMOD, is an ice-and-rock avalanche that blocked the Lhende Khola inside Tibet about 20 km upstream of the Miteri Bridge, forming a temporary debris-dammed lake that then burst. The US Geological Survey has reassessed the tremor recorded minutes earlier as a magnitude 5.2 glacial collapse, not an earthquake. Heavy local rainfall in Rasuwa is ruled out. Full explanation.

    Which districts of Nepal were affected?

    Rasuwa took the first and heaviest impact, especially Timure, Syabrubesi and the Rasuwagadhi border crossing. The surge then moved through Betrawati and Trishuli Bazar in Nuwakot, Galchhi and Dhunge Bazaar in Dhading, and past Muglin into the Narayani. Bodies have been recovered in Chitwan, Nawalparasi East and West, Gorkha and Tanahun as well. Three deaths are confirmed on the Chinese side near Gyirong.

    Is Kathmandu affected?

    Kathmandu itself was not flooded. The damage runs along the Bhotekoshi and Trishuli river valleys in Rasuwa and Nuwakot districts, north-west of the capital. Road links to the Chinese border through Rasuwagadhi have been badly disrupted.

    What are the emergency numbers in Nepal?

    Nepal Police 100, ambulance 102, fire brigade 101, traffic police 103, and 112 from any mobile phone. The National Emergency Operation Centre disaster hotline is 1149, the Nepal Police toll-free helpline is 1155, and Tourist Police is 1144. All of them are tap-to-dial on the Emergency tab.

    How can I donate to Nepal flood relief?

    The Government of Nepal's own channel is the Prime Minister's Natural Disaster Relief Fund, run by the Office of the Prime Minister. Bank account details and the official QR code are on the Help tab. Be careful of fake appeals, which appear within hours of any disaster.

    Is it safe to travel to Langtang or Syabrubesi now?

    Syabrubesi is the main gateway to Langtang trekking and was directly hit, including the loss of its helipad. Roads along the river valley have been damaged. Check with Nepal Police, the Tourist Police on 1144, and your trekking operator before travelling.

    Why here, and why so fast

    Three things about this valley that decided how bad it got

    Why this valley floods hard

    The river drops about 3,000 metres in under 40 km, one of the steepest drops in the Himalaya. Water from a burst upstream can reach a village in under an hour. Almost no time to run.

    One river, two names

    Above the join it is called Bhotekoshi. Below it, Trishuli. Same water. In Tibet the upper river is the Kyirong Tsangpo, fed by the Lende Khola, where the ice and rock came down.

    It happened here before

    A broadly similar flood hit this same river in July 2025, traced to a lake draining off the surface of a glacier. Hydropower on this corridor has now been damaged twice in just over a year.

    01

    Call the nearest District Police Office or 100. Give full name, age, last known location, and what they were wearing.

    02

    Register the same details with the local ward office, they hold the evacuation and shelter lists.

    If the person may have crossed into China at Rasuwagadhi, tell the police that specifically. Cross-border checks are handled separately.

    Help that isn't money

    Blood is needed after any disaster; the Nepal Red Cross runs collection across Kathmandu. Verified local photos, missing-person details and shelter reports also help, they are what response teams work from.

    Before you donate anywhere else

    Fake relief appeals appear within hours of every disaster. Check the account name matches a registered organisation, be suspicious of personal wallets, and never send money because a message says the deadline is tonight.

    Learn

    Nepal's natural disasters, explained simply

    Nepal sits where two continents collide, under monsoon rain and below melting ice. Five hazards come out of that. Which one you are facing changes what you should do.

    Glacial lake outburst flood (GLOF): what hit Rasuwa

    High in the mountains, melting glaciers leave lakes behind. The wall holding one back is often just loose rock and ice. When it fails, the whole lake leaves at once and races down the valley as a wall of water, mud, and boulders.

    Warning signs: the river suddenly rises or suddenly stops, on a day with no rain. A roar from upstream. Water turning grey or brown.

    What to do: go uphill, away from the river, immediately. Not along the road, roads follow rivers. You may have minutes, not hours.

    Nepal's exposure: more than 20 glacial lakes are classified as potentially dangerous, and many more sit across the border in Tibet where Nepal has no monitoring.

    Earthquakes

    India is pushing under Asia at about 2 cm a year. That strain releases as earthquakes. The 2015 Gorkha earthquake killed nearly 9,000 people.

    What to do during shaking: drop, cover, hold on. Get under a sturdy table, away from windows. Do not run outside mid-shake, falling masonry is the main killer.

    After: expect aftershocks. Avoid damaged buildings, steep slopes and riverbanks, a quake loosens both.

    Landslides and debris flows

    Steep slopes plus heavy rain plus cut roads equals landslides. They cause a large share of Nepal's monsoon deaths every year, usually at night, usually with no warning.

    Warning signs: new cracks in the ground or in walls, doors and windows suddenly sticking, tilting trees or poles, a rumbling that grows.

    What to do: move sideways off the slope path, not straight downhill. If indoors on a slope at night in heavy rain, sleep on the uphill side of the house.

    Monsoon river floods

    June to September brings most of Nepal's yearly rain. In the Tarai plains, rivers spread out slowly over days; in the hills they rise in hours.

    What to do: never walk or drive into moving water. Thirty centimetres will float a car, fifteen will knock an adult over. Turn around.

    Watch: the BIPAD portal publishes live river gauge levels for major rivers.

    Fire, lightning and cold waves

    Less talked about, still deadly. Lightning kills around 100 people a year in Nepal. Dry-season fires spread fast through village housing; winter cold waves hit the Tarai hardest.

    Lightning rule: when you hear thunder within 30 seconds of a flash, go inside for 30 minutes after the last thunder. Not under a lone tree.

    If you live near a mountain river, do this

    01

    Know a route uphill, not along the road. Roads follow rivers, and they flood first.

    02

    Loud river noise, or water rising with no rain, is the warning. Move first, ask questions later.

    03

    Keep ID, cash, medicines and a torch in one bag you can grab in the dark.

    04

    Pick a family meeting spot above the flood line. Phones stop working first.

    05

    If you run a shop or homestay by the river, plan to lose the ground floor. Know what's load-bearing.

    06

    Save the emergency numbers on the next tab into your phone now, not during the emergency.

    The warning-time problem

    A burst lake can reach a village in under an hour. Some Nepali lakes now have sensors and sirens. Most, especially those across the border, do not, so the first warning is often the sound of the river itself.

    Why it is getting worse

    Himalayan ice is melting faster than it is replaced. More meltwater means more and bigger lakes held back by weaker walls, in valleys where more people now live and build than a generation ago.
